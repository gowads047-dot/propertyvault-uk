6. Circumstances for Deposit Deductions

The Landlord may make reasonable deductions from the deposit for:

  • Unpaid rent or other sums due under the tenancy agreement
  • Damage to the property or its contents caused by the Tenant (beyond fair wear and tear)
  • Missing items belonging to the Landlord
  • Cleaning costs to return the property to the standard recorded in the check-in inventory
  • Any other breach of the tenancy agreement resulting in financial loss to the Landlord

7. Deposit Return

At the end of the tenancy, the deposit (or the balance after any agreed deductions) will be returned to the Tenant within 10 days of both parties agreeing the amount to be returned.

8. Dispute Resolution

If the Landlord and Tenant cannot agree on the amount to be returned, either party may refer the dispute to the free Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) service provided by the deposit protection scheme. The decision of the ADR service is binding on both parties. Alternatively, either party may take the dispute to the County Court.
